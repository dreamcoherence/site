The Work

A dream never feels like a dream. It feels like this.

Zhuang Zhou dreamed he was a butterfly, fluttering freely, with no thought of the man. Then he woke, and there the man was. And he could not say whether he was a man who had dreamed the butterfly, or a butterfly now dreaming the man. This is called the transformation of things.Zhuangzi · fourth century BC

The Recognition

Everything you have ever known has appeared within awareness.

Not as an idea. As the plainest fact of experience.

Begin there.

Stay with it, and something else becomes impossible to ignore. The same morning, met with wonder or with worry, is a different morning. The morning has not changed. The way you met it has.

Experience comes to feel less like something happening to you, and more like something happening with you. The world comes to feel less like a machine, and more like a dream.

The Golden Thread

A life is closer to a melody than a plan.

Certain notes keep sounding: a love, a longing, a labor, a question you never finish answering. Heard alone, each is only a note. Heard together, they are one theme, finding its harmony.

Mastery is learning to hear what is being composed, and to play it deliberately.

True Freedom

The world remains vivid. The body remains human. Love, work, mystery, and tension remain.

What changes is the way life can move through you.

Some call this integration liberated while living. Awake in the dream, and dreaming it well.

Learning to dream it well is the practice →

Practice

How does reality uniquely wish to become itself through this life?

Attention is where the answer begins to clarify.

By day, what is shaping you.
At evening, what the night inherits.
In dream, recognition in another form.
On waking, what you make of it.

This is the practice: to become the continuity that waking, dream, and sleep move within.

About

This is one dreaming, walking a path of remembrance.

What it gathers is offered back to the mystery that moves through us all, so the dreaming might grow lucid in more of us, and we might tend these worlds with grace. The way it cultivates abides through love and work and ordinary days: the dream is the dream is the dream.

Our practice arises from the confluence of nondual Kashmir Shaivism, Dzogchen and the dream and sleep yogas of the Bön tradition, Advaita Vedanta’s realization of Turiya, Daoist internal alchemy, the Sufi path of the heart, the mystical cartography of Kabbalah, and the flower-and-song of the Nahua sages. It is further informed by modern explorers of consciousness and the imaginal, including William James, Carl Jung, Henry Corbin, Peter Kingsley and others, while remaining attentive to the growing consilience between these perennial insights and the leading edge of scientific inquiry.
